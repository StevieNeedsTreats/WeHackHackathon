Every claim, traced back to its sources in one graph

A question splits into hypotheses. Papers are searched for each hypothesis, and their studies and findings are extracted. Each finding then supports or refutes a hypothesis. FalkorDB stores all of it as nodes and relationships, so following evidence from a verdict back to whoever funded it is a single query.

Why this data lives in a graph

The relationship carries the meaning

Whether a finding supports or refutes a hypothesis is stored on the EVIDENCE_FOR edge, as stance and weight. The same finding can support one hypothesis and refute another without being copied.

Many hops in one query

The funding trail goes from a question to its hypotheses, findings, studies, papers and funders. That's five hops in a single MATCH pattern. In SQL, every hop would be another join through a link table.

Paths of any length

CITES*1..3 follows citation chains up to three papers deep with one pattern. In SQL you'd need a recursive CTE for the same thing.

Research is reused

Every write is a MERGE on a unique key, and papers are deduplicated by DOI, PMID and normalised title. A new question that matches earlier research joins nodes already in the graph, so nothing is looked up twice. Concept synonyms extend this to reworded questions: “lean mass” resolves to the existing muscle mass concept.

Indexed where it's queried

FalkorDB indexes what the app searches: full text on concept names and paper titles, and relationship indexes on CONTRADICTS.hypothesis and MEASURES.hypothesis so each hypothesis's links are found directly.

Safe to explore live

Writes are batched with UNWIND and parameterised, so FalkorDB reuses cached query plans. The in-app Cypher console runs through GRAPH.RO_QUERY with a timeout and a row cap, so anyone can explore but nobody can change the data.
